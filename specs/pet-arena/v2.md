# Pet Arena v2 Product And Contract Specification

Status: design candidate, OFFLINE_ONLY, NO_GO_PUBLIC. This is a product/contract
specification, not another backlog. Task state and dependencies live only in
`config/development-plan.json`. No v2 runtime, migration or publication is claimed
by this document. Automated balance checks cannot establish that combat is fun.

## Compatibility And Ownership

The audited base is main `1178ba5ebd599f86a299eec246d65cb70828c4b1`.
VL-113 remains a separate in-progress Linux milestone on PR 27; this track must
not complete it implicitly. Existing Rust training content has eight Pets, six
stages, a 6x6 board, four elemental tiles and a frozen v1 digest. Keep its bytes,
fixtures, reducer codec and historical receipts intact. Do not map v1 tile IDs
to the new functional tile IDs.

Introduce ruleset `pet-arena-pve-v2`, intent kind `pet.intent.v2`, view kind
`pet.view.v2`, completion kind `pet-arena.completed.v2` and event schema 2.
Allocate distinct content and adapter hashes, with an explicit versioned decoder.
Keep existing protobuf field numbers and v1 consumers; new fields are additive.
Old clients fail closed on unknown schema. Never upgrade an active match in place.
Importing a v1 training Pet to v2 is a separate opt-in entitlement mapping with
a durable conversion receipt; v1 fixtures grant no collection entitlement.

Rust owns combat, board/RNG, damage, resource changes and terminal outcomes.
NestJS owns identity, policy, admission, content publication, progression
transactions and platform settlement. PostgreSQL uses the existing Nest migration
journal (base ends at 0050); choose the next migration number from live HEAD
when implementing, not from this specification. Reuse native encrypted journal,
single Host writer, receipts and outbox/inbox. Rust gets only reviewed table and
column grants; it does not migrate schemas or write the platform reward ledger.
Unity sends intent and presents bounded owner projections and typed events.

## Elements And Collection Scope

The owner selected 19 elements: normal, fire, water, grass, electric, ice,
fighting, poison, ground, flying, psychic, bug, rock, ghost, dragon, dark,
steel, fairy and light. Earlier Earth means ground; Wind means flying. These
are aliases at content import only, not additional combat types. v1 Ember/Tide/
Grove/Spark stay in the v1 namespace. Begin with one element per species; dual
elements require a separately reviewed rules version.

`catalog-v2.json` authors 114 original species candidates, six per element,
across 19 biomes. Stable IDs, silhouettes, roles, generic loadouts, stat profiles,
level ranges, capture and unlock gates are explicit. These are metadata designs:
none has approved visual assets, balance acceptance or publication rights.
Initial implementation seeds at least 24 definitions; polished presentation
starts with one hero for each of the original six elements (fire, water, ground,
flying, light, dark) and one boss. The other thirteen elements can use visibly
marked development previews until their own assets pass. No existing game names,
characters, sprites, sounds, UI or proprietary content may be reused.

The elemental matrix uses attack rows and defense columns. Neutral = 1000,
advantage = 1250, resistance = 800 and explicit immunity = 0, all permille.
The catalog records every non-neutral relation. Incoming weaknesses are derived
from attack rows, never maintained as a conflicting second list. Normal has no
advantage. Electric cannot damage ground; normal/fighting cannot damage ghost.
UI must show immunity and offer alternate allowed elemental or utility actions;
each published loadout must have at least one legal response to every encounter.
Light is strong against dark/ghost, resisted by grass/steel/light; dark gains
advantage over light, steel/grass also threaten light. These additions are
candidate balance rules; every element must have counters and none dominates
all rows. Critical chance and rarity cannot erase elemental bounds.

## Shared Combat v2

The board remains 6x6, with six equally identifiable functional categories:
sword, mana, rage, heart, shield and special. The engine generates a playable
initial board without unresolved matches, guarantees a legal move or performs
a recorded deterministic reshuffle. Adjacent orthogonal swaps must make a match.
Illegal swaps/stale versions spend no turn, resources or RNG. Initial cap: 16
cascades, 60 rounds and bounded per-turn event output. Reaching the cascade cap
ends resolution deterministically; never rely on client animation completion.

One player action, complete cascade resolution, then one telegraphed enemy
response forms a round. No unbounded extra turns. Legal actions: swap, normal,
rage, skill, card, combo, defend and pass. Normal is always available and spends
the action; sword matches add attacks during a swap action. Mana/rage caps are
100 each, shield cap is 50% maximum HP, health cannot exceed maximum HP. Draft
per matched tile: sword 250 permille attack, mana +5, rage +4, heart 200 permille
attack healing, shield 200 permille attack protection. A length-4 match has
1250 permille yield and length >=5 has 1500; cascade multiplier grows by 100
permille per cascade to 1500 maximum. Apply integer floors once per defined
stage, and reject overflow. A special match grants one combo charge (cap 3)
and 5 mana; it does not execute user-defined effects.

Stat profiles define HP/attack/defense/speed coefficients with a fixed total
budget. Growth curves are shared and capped at level 100. Species coefficients
are bounded to 800..1200 permille and must preserve that budget; rarity affects
availability/appearance, not an automatic damage multiplier. Draft base stats
at level 1: HP 100, attack 20, defense 15, speed 10. Effective stat = floor(base
* roleCoefficient/1000 * (1000 + growthPermille*(level-1))/1000), then apply a
published bounded upgrade factor. Speed controls deterministic initiative only,
with stable server participant ordering as the tie-breaker.

Draft damage formula: attack * actionPower/1000 * element/1000 * critical/1000
* 100/(100+defense), with checked fixed-point arithmetic, minimum 1 for positive
nonimmune damage and mitigation caps of 75%. Critical chance max 250 permille,
critical multiplier max 1500, and total temporary attack buffs max +50%.
Apply shield before HP. DOT has explicit duration, max stacks and immunity rules;
no indefinite stun or status renewal loop. Telegraphs expose targets and timing,
never private RNG state. RNG seed/cursor stays in authenticated server storage.

Rage needs 100 energy, consumes it once and uses the shared charged strike
(draft power 2000). Skills use bounded engine enum IDs, not scripts. Initial
catalog: strike, guard, mend, poison, cleanse, empower, break_guard, slow,
channel and siphon. Each has reviewed costs, cooldown, target class, stack cap
and effect opcode list. Cards equip at most two allowed definitions; an equipped
card is reusable on cooldown. Consumables, if later enabled, debit one durable
inventory item. Combos name two compatible equipped skills, cost two charges
and 40 mana, set both cooldowns and emit one atomic action. No arbitrary nested
effects, SQL, URLs, expressions, reflection names or executable code in content.

Events carry stable match ID, committed version, event ordinal, actor/target
public handles, cue enum and bounded public values. Event ID is derived from
match/version/ordinal, so replay animates at most once. Event vocabulary includes
action_started, tiles_matched, tiles_fell, resource_changed, normal_hit,
rage_hit, skill_cast, combo_cast, shield_absorbed, status_changed, defeated,
victory and settlement_changed. Private viewer allowlists, scalar/depth/duplicate
key checks and immutable pending-command handling apply to v2 as to v1.

## Capture, Achievements And Progression

Capture defaults to three distinct eligible victories for the encounter family,
recorded as 0/3, 1/3, 2/3, complete. Unique key includes account, encounter,
capture cycle and authoritative completion receipt. Loss, abandoned or practice
matches add zero; replay and settlement retries add zero. Grant one Pet instance
in the same transaction as reaching 3/3, with a unique acquisition receipt.
Progress has no daily reset or inactivity expiry. Retirement preserves earned
progress; offer a published compatible replacement encounter or keep the retired
completion route. Never silently reset progress on balance publication. Repeated
capture cycles require an explicit new server-issued cycle after completion;
bosses unlock collection after campaign clearance, separate from weekly loot caps.

Achievements are permanent, receipt-backed points from eligible first clears
and bounded repeat objectives. Access checks use current server policy plus
published achievement thresholds and prerequisite clear receipts. Repeating a
first-clear receipt cannot increase points. UI shows unmet gates without leaking
private peer state. Owned Pet XP and levels persist across logout/restart.
Level growth cannot modify a match's frozen stat snapshot after allocation.

Upgrade tiers 0..10 use published probabilities in permille:
1000, 900, 800, 700, 600, 500, 400, 300, 250, 200. Tier 1 is a tutorial;
later tiers carry actual failure risk. Show exact odds and materials before
confirmation. Failure consumes the advertised materials once, retains the Pet,
level and existing tier; no hidden downgrade or destruction. No paid advantage.
No implicit pity. A later protection feature needs a new published explicit rule.
Server records immutable attempt ID, content version, preconditions, RNG draw
commitment/result and resource ledger entries. Serialize upgrades per owned Pet
and account materials, use checked nonnegative balances and replay the same
receipt on lost ACK. Neither retries nor DB rollback may reroll the attempt.

## Database And Publication Contract

Add normalized relations through additive migrations. Names below are proposed;
final migration must reconcile ownership config and live schema:

| Domain | Relations And Invariants |
| --- | --- |
| Content | pet_content_versions(id, parent, schema, ruleset, hash, state); immutable published manifests |
| Species | pet_species_revisions(version, species_id, element_id, rarity_id, growth_id, form_id); composite foreign keys |
| Balance | pet_elements, pet_effectiveness, pet_growth_profiles, pet_upgrade_curves; bounded numeric checks |
| Abilities | pet_skill_definitions, pet_card_definitions, pet_combinations, pet_loadouts; enum opcodes only |
| Encounters | pet_maps, pet_encounters, pet_capture_rules, pet_unlock_rules, pet_boss_rotations; authoritative schedules |
| Assets | pet_asset_manifests, pet_visual_forms, pet_animation_bindings; immutable hashes and provenance |
| Ownership | account_pet_instances, pet_experience_receipts, pet_equipment; account ownership enforced |
| Progress | pet_capture_cycles, pet_capture_receipts, pet_achievement_receipts; unique completion receipt keys |
| Upgrades | pet_upgrade_attempts, pet_material_balances, pet_material_ledger; one balanced transactional debit |
| Social | pet_assistance_invites, pet_assistance_receipts; explicit acceptance, expiry and reward caps |
| Seasons | pet_seasons, pet_boss_participation, pet_ranking_entries; unique settlement and stable tie-breakers |
| Audit | pet_content_audit; operator/action/before/after/time, no secrets |

Do not duplicate platform monetary/reward ownership. Extend approved native
completion proposal and worker policy contracts for nonmonetary progression;
worker validates actor, ruleset/content, receipt, eligibility and policy again.
Inbox dedupe, capture receipt, achievement, XP and Pet grant commit atomically.
External visual blobs belong in immutable approved bundles/store; SQL stores
hashes, size/type, rights state, compatibility and source references, not blobs.

Draft -> validated preview -> published -> retired. Draft edits use expected
revision. Validation produces a hash-bound report; publish rechecks dependencies,
rights and policy transactionally. Published rows cannot be edited/deleted.
Rollback selects a previously compatible published version for future allocation,
not a rewrite of active matches. A match pins ruleset hash, content hash,
loadouts, stat snapshot and encounter revision. Retired species remain owned
and historical receipts remain decodable. Restore tests cover wrong key, missing/
corrupt journal, previous schemas, content manifest absence and no duplicate grants.

## Operator Pet Studio

Extend existing admin-web operations modules, the existing admin proxy allowlist
and Nest operator sessions/permissions. Reuse current MFA step-up, CSRF, Origin,
rate limits, idempotency and durable audit patterns. Permissions are distinct:
pet.read, pet.draft.write, pet.preview, pet.publish, pet.retire and pet.rollback.
Publishing, retirement, rollback and economy changes require fresh step-up and
explicit confirmation naming exact version/hash and player impact.

Screens: catalog table/filter, definition editor, duplicate-as-draft, element
matrix, shared skills/cards, progression curves, encounter/map gates, asset
bindings, preview results, version diff and audit. Preview runs a bounded
isolated server simulation and Unity content bundle; it cannot mutate live match
state or grant player rewards. First implementation is one database-backed Pet
draft/read/edit/preview with authorization negatives, concurrency and audit.
Subsequent publishing adds immutable manifests; no Pet-specific Rust redeploy.

Production publication fails for missing/wrong-hash/unlicensed assets, invalid
skill IDs, impossible level curves, duplicate identifiers, missing animation
semantics, broken element refs or an immunity softlock. Development content can
remain draft with clearly labeled placeholders and never become production-ready
merely because metadata was seeded.

Each published equipped card binds an allowed attack element in its revision;
the player cannot send a replacement element. Every starting loadout includes
the reusable elemental-strike card with an approved nonimmune alternate element.
The content validator simulates every encounter/loadout pair and rejects a
damage immunity softlock before publication. Draft metadata does not imply this
runtime validation exists yet.

## Presentation And Qualification

Use original tile sprite atlas with selected/matched/falling/warning/disabled/
triggered variants. Shared Pet animation semantics: idle, summon, normal, rage,
skill, combo, hurt, defend, status, defeat and victory. Asset manifest includes
editable source/provenance, importer profile, prefab/controller, clips/timeline,
atlas/Addressables group, material/shader variant, VFX/audio bindings, localization,
content hash and minimum runtime schema. Models/portraits are not authored by
this specification; every species currently has assets MISSING.

Unity queues/deduplicates authoritative event IDs. Fast-forward, skip, reduced
motion, unavailable VFX and slow animation must produce identical canonical
results. Inputs remain bounded during animation; server deadlines never wait
for a timeline. Readable board, HP/mana/rage/shield, cards/cooldowns, statuses,
telegraphs, capture progress, odds and settlement precede cinematic effects.
Avoid full-screen flashes and forced long camera sequences. Provide separate
camera shake, speed, motion and audio settings.

Linux initial target profile: Ryzen 5 5600, RX 6600 8GB, 16GB RAM, SSD, 1080p
medium at 60 FPS; candidate budget CPU/GPU p95 <=16.7ms, p99 <=25ms, process
RSS <=2GB and warm battle load <=3s. These are targets, not measurements.
Record actual device/driver/preset, CPU/GPU timings, shader hitches, effect cost,
animation latency and loading/memory on named hardware; lower presets must keep
board readability. Physical Windows URP/IL2CPP qualification, source asset rights,
signing and public release remain separate gates. Primitive previews prove no
production art quality. Real consented playtests cover comprehension, fairness,
upgrade odds and enjoyment; do not synthesize human acceptance.

## Social And Seasonal Scope

Reuse identity/friend/consent/block services. Assistance requires explicit invite
acceptance, current eligibility and reward caps. Stronger-player assistance has
published level normalization and limited rewards, no account boosting loophole.
Weekly boss rotation pins version/schedule/eligibility; unique account/week/
milestone reward receipts prevent retry farming. Season rankings use verified
authoritative scores, score descending, earliest qualifying commit then stable
opaque tie-breaker; expose opted-in display identity only. Withdrawal hides public
identity without erasing integrity evidence beyond approved retention policy.
Ranked/unranked PvP implementation follows PvE progression and balance validation;
it cannot expose RNG or bypass moderation during reconnect.

## Verification Contract

Required acceptance probes map to plan tasks, not claims in this document:
three unique wins -> exactly one capture; duplicate receipt -> zero progress;
inventory survives logout/restart; upgrade result/debit once across lost ACK;
admin publish new species without Pet-specific code; generic element/loadout
handling; distinct normal/rage events; animation skip leaves same result;
replay/invalid/stale/wrong actor/duplicate settlement negatives; publication
cannot change active match; missing/unlicensed asset denial; capped assistance/
boss rewards; real rendered Linux progression; frozen v1 replay unchanged.

Source validation requires governance, catalog cross-reference checks, relevant
Rust/TS/database/admin/protocol tests and hosted exact-source Quality/Rust.
Rendered Linux and Windows hardware evidence are recorded separately, with
actual commands/exits, source revision and hashes under ignored artifacts.
Incremental order is the task DAG: P0 specification, P1 catalog/admin foundation,
P2 generic combat, P3 capture/progression, P4 polished vertical slice, P5 authored
catalog, P6 social/bosses, P7 balance/security/qualification. Art architecture can
start at P0; it does not depend on social feature completion.
